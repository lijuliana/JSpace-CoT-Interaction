### Summary

The paper asks whether a value a language model writes during chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is causally used by later computation, or merely narrates work done elsewhere. Using arithmetic and narrative state-tracking tasks with known ground truth, the authors run three interventions on five to seven open-weight models (plus three frontier models via API prefill): (1) planting an unwritten value into the residual stream at a written token's position, which the final answer then tracks (0.74–1.00 across families); (2) blocking attention from later positions to that token, which collapses "edit-following" to ~0 while leaving unrelated attention blocks unaffected; and (3) testing four conditions under which models do or don't revert to the correct value when prompt evidence conflicts with an edited written value, finding model-specific "checking" policies. The headline claim is that the written token's residual-stream state functions as addressable memory that later computation reads through attention, and that stronger models rely on this memory more, not less — a claim with direct implications for CoT-based oversight.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The abstract and introduction present the central causal question ("does the written token carry the computation?") as unresolved before this paper, but the paper's own §7 reveals that arXiv:2606.29522 already answers it affirmatively via near-identical representation-patching, with comparable downstream-following effect sizes (0.80–0.91).
   **Where** — Abstract ("We answer this causally…"); Introduction §1 ("Faithfulness studies have shown the two can disagree, but disagreement in specific cases leaves the general mechanism open"); contrast with §7's "Closest to this work, arXiv:2606.29522 edits internal representations of scratchpad state and finds downstream following at 0.80 to 0.91."
   **Why it matters** — If a prior paper already demonstrated that patched, unwritten scratchpad values causally determine downstream answers at a similar magnitude, then the paper's most citable claim — that it is the first causal demonstration of this mechanism — is not accurate; only the *scope* (three model families, unmodified pretrained models, attention localization, composition, checking-policy mapping) is new, not the core causal fact. Readers who only see the abstract will over-attribute priority. This is exactly the kind of claim inflation that damages a field's ability to track who established what.
   **What would address it** — Reframe the abstract/intro to state precisely what is new relative to arXiv:2606.29522 and arXiv:2505.04955 (generality across untouched pretrained models and families, attention-level localization with matched controls, compositionality, checking-policy taxonomy) rather than implying the base causal fact was previously unknown. Move the closest-work comparison earlier, ideally into the introduction, not deferred to §7.

2. **Issue** — The paper's second pillar, that attention to the written token is the specific causal conduit ("Blocking attention…collapses edit-following…drops edit-following to zero"), is inadequately differentiated from arXiv:2606.10646, which the paper itself describes as already showing that "attention-flow perturbation on reasoning traces…flips answers by perturbing flow hubs." Separately, §6's scope-boundary result rests on an "Jacobian lens" ablation that is never cited or described methodologically.
   **Where** — §4 (attention-block results); §7 ("the attention block is related to attention knockout in circuit analysis and to attention-flow perturbation on reasoning traces (arXiv:2606.10646), which flips answers by perturbing flow hubs; we add value-specific edges with matched-token-type controls and the illegible-token dissociation"); §6 ("Ablating the top concepts a Jacobian lens reads from the activations…").
   **Why it matters** — "Flow hubs" and "the written value's token" may or may not be the same locus described differently; without a head-to-head comparison or explicit definition of how these differ, a reader cannot tell whether §4's central result is a genuinely new localization or a relabeled instance of an already-published finding. Separately, introducing "a Jacobian lens" with zero citation or method description in the one place the paper argues for a scope *limit* to its own mechanism makes that boundary claim unverifiable and possibly borrowed from unattributed prior work.
   **What would address it** — Directly compare "flow hubs" (2606.10646) against the written-value token position (e.g., overlap statistics, or an explicit statement that they coincide/differ), and cite/describe the Jacobian-lens method (source, what it reads, how "top concepts" are selected, what "strongest dose that leaves plain text generation unchanged" means quantitatively).

3. **Issue** — The two-slot composition result, arguably the single strongest piece of evidence against a "generic disruption" alternative explanation, is numerically unverifiable and internally inconsistent between the abstract and the body.
   **Where** — Abstract ("appears nowhere in its input, in 88 percent of cases"; "at or above the product of the single-position rates"); §3 ("producing their sum (0.90 and 0.88 on Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B), matching the product of the single-slot rates").
   **Why it matters** — "At or above the product" (abstract) and "matching the product" (body) are different claims, and neither can be checked because the single-slot baseline rate for this specific two-counter task is never reported anywhere in the text (Table 3's 0.74/1.00 figures are for the unrelated ten-step arithmetic-chain task). If the relevant single-slot rate is, say, 0.90–0.95 (plausible given other single-slot rates in the paper), then the product would be ~0.81–0.90, arguably consistent with "matching," but if it is closer to the 0.74 figure quoted elsewhere, the product (~0.55) would be far below the observed 0.88–0.90, i.e., "above," not "matching," and would actually argue against clean independence (positive dependence between slots, which would undercut the "independent addressable slots" framing). As written, the reader cannot tell which regime holds, yet this result carries much of the paper's mechanistic weight.
   **What would address it** — Report the single-slot planted-value rate for the same two-counter task (with the same n and CIs as the composition rate), and reconcile the "at or above" vs. "matching" language.

4. **Issue** — The papers doing the most novelty-differentiating work (arXiv:2606.29522, arXiv:2505.04955, arXiv:2606.10646, arXiv:2604.23351, arXiv:2602.15868, arXiv:2603.01437, arXiv:2412.01113) are cited only by bare ID, with no author names or titles, unlike the paper's other references (Merrill and Sabharwal 2024; Meng et al. 2022; Turpin et al. 2023; Lanham et al. 2023; Lanchantin et al. 2023; Pfau et al. 2024), which are given standard author-year citations.
   **Where** — §7 throughout.
   **Why it matters** — This asymmetry makes it materially harder for a reader or reviewer to locate, cross-check, and weigh these specific comparisons — precisely the ones on which the novelty argument depends. It also raises the more basic question of whether these citations were independently verified for correctness of characterization (e.g., that 2606.29522 really is "a Qwen-7B fine-tuned for a synthetic task" with "downstream following at 0.80 to 0.91") before being used to stake out the paper's contribution boundary.
   **What would address it** — Add full bibliographic entries (authors, title, venue/year) for every arXiv-ID-only citation, consistent with the rest of the reference list, and, ideally, a short table contrasting task, model, and effect size across the three or four closest prior works alongside this paper's own numbers.

5. **Issue** — Sample sizes are small and uneven across the many models and fine-grained conditions on which the paper's central taxonomy ("when do models check the written record") rests, with no correction for the number of comparisons made.
   **Where** — Table in §3 (R1-distill-14B: n=46; R1-distill-7B: n=65; R1-distill-1.5B excluded with only 12 usable items); §2 ("Sample sizes are 100 to 600 per condition with 2 to 3 seeds"); §8 discussion frontier comparison ("R1-distill-7B 0.42, DeepSeek V3.2 0.78, Claude Sonnet 4.5 0.97") given with no n or CI anywhere in the main text.
   **Why it matters** — The paper draws a specific, model-by-model behavioral taxonomy (DeepSeek reverts only when generatively defined; Sonnet reverts most when sole-source; Llama almost never checks; Qwen never checks) from differences like 0.43 vs. 0.05, or 0.78 vs. 0.25–0.32, computed on samples in the same 46–600 range, across many pairwise contrasts, without any stated correction for multiple comparisons. For the smallest cells (e.g., n=46, or the 12-item excluded 1.5B case), single-digit-percent differences are within plausible sampling noise, yet are used to support qualitative, cross-model claims ("Capacity to rederive is necessary for checking; whether it is used is a property of the model's training").
   **What would address it** — Report n and CIs for every number cited in §5 and §8 (not just the tables in §3–4), pre-register or at least flag which contrasts are confirmatory vs. exploratory, and apply (or justify not applying) a multiple-comparison adjustment given the number of model × condition cells compared.

6. **Issue** — The state-patch intervention, which overwrites "a middle band of layers" (a broad, non-isolated set of activations) rather than a value-specific subspace, cannot fully rule out that the observed "following" effect reflects a generic anomalous-representation-triggers-completion mechanism rather than genuine transplantation of a discrete, addressable numeric value.
   **Where** — §2 ("the state patch…overwrites the residual stream at the value's token position across a middle band of layers with activations captured from a run in which a different value was written there"); acknowledged only briefly in §8 Limitations ("the patch overwrites the full residual band rather than an isolated value subspace").
   **Why it matters** — Because the patch transplants a whole captured activation bundle (including whatever upstream conditioning produced it in a full alternate run) rather than an isolated "value" feature, the norm-matched random-vector control rules out generic magnitude-of-perturbation effects but does not rule out that the model is responding to "this position now looks like it belongs to a different, coherent computational trajectory" rather than reading a stored scalar per se. Given that this concern is only substantively addressed by the compositionality result (concern 3, above, which is itself numerically unresolved), the paper's strongest rebuttal to the alternative explanation is weaker than presented.
   **What would address it** — Attempt a narrower patch (a small number of layers, or a lens-identified subspace) and show the effect survives at comparable magnitude; alternatively, provide a dose-response curve over patch bandwidth to demonstrate the effect is not simply proportional to how much of the "different-value run" context is transplanted.

### Minor concerns

- "Reversion" (§5, §8) is used throughout with specific decimal values (0.43, 0.78, 0.90, etc.) but is never formally defined (e.g., is it 1 − following-rate, or a rate over a distinct outcome category that could include neither the edited nor the correct value?). This should be defined with an equation or explicit operationalization.
- OLMo-2-7B's attention-block baseline is already low (0.333) before blocking, so the "collapse" to 0.005 is a smaller absolute swing than for other models (e.g., 0.900→0.000 for Qwen3-4B); the parenthetical "whose contrast is intact" asserts but doesn't demonstrate that this isn't partly a floor effect.
- The abstract states "88 percent" for the two-slot composition result as a single figure, while §3 reports two different values (0.90 and 0.88) for two different models; the abstract should indicate this is a range or specify which model.
- §6's "box-tracking task with equal state size is tracked internally with a fifth of the writing, and writing does not predict its correctness" is asserted with no accompanying numbers, table, or n — this entire scope-boundary claim is currently unverifiable from the text.
- The claim that R1-distill models' "re-solving can only produce the correct answer, so their planted-value rates are unaffected by it" is asserted rather than shown; a brief quantitative check (e.g., comparing planted-value rates with and without the re-solving phase) would strengthen this.
- Figure references ("[Figure: knockout bars]", "[Figure: four-condition reversion, V3.2 and Sonnet]") are placeholders with no captions, axis definitions, or error bars described in text — cannot be evaluated as submitted.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the causal evidence (planting, attention-blocking, composition) is a genuinely valuable multi-family result, but the novelty framing overstates priority relative to the paper's own cited closest work, key supporting numbers (composition, scope-boundary) are either internally inconsistent or unreported, and closest-prior-art citations lack the bibliographic detail needed to adjudicate the contribution claims.
