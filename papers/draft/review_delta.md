## Summary

The paper asks whether an intermediate value a language model writes during chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is causally used by later computation, or is merely post-hoc commentary. Using arithmetic addition/subtraction chains and a narrative marble/stamp-counting analogue with known ground truth, the authors apply three interventions — visible text edits, residual-stream "state patches" that plant an unwritten value at a written token's position, and attention knockouts that block later positions from attending to that token — across six white-box models (Qwen3-4B, Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-3-medium, OLMo-2-7B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill 7B/14B) and three frontier models probed behaviorally through API prefill (DeepSeek V3.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Llama 3.3 70B). The headline results: planted values are adopted in the final answer 74–100% of the time versus <1% for norm-matched random controls; blocking attention to the written token collapses this effect to ~0 while matched control blocks (adjacent tokens, random operands, earlier values) do not; two independently planted values compose additively; and models differ sharply in whether/when they check the written record against prompt evidence, with the paper arguing this checking behavior — not the underlying memory mechanism — is what varies with training.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — A headline "oversight" statistic in the Introduction appears numerically inconsistent with the same model's statistics reported in Section 5, undermining confidence in the paper's central strength-scaling claim.
   **Where** — Section 1: "edit-following on such values increases with model strength (R1-distill-7B 0.42, DeepSeek V3.2 0.78, Claude Sonnet 4.5 0.97...)" versus Section 5: DeepSeek V3.2's reported values are 0.43 (defined condition), 0.01 (redundant checkpoint), 0.05 (sole-source checkpoint), 0.85–0.958 (deep/one-op-away conditions) — nowhere does 0.78 appear for V3.2. Instead, 0.78 is explicitly Claude Sonnet 4.5's reversion rate in the sole-source-checkpoint condition ("adding a sole-source checkpoint raises its reversion from 0.18 to 0.78"). Similarly, "R1-distill-7B 0.42" does not match Table 1's R1-distill-7B text-edit-followed rate (0.60); it is close to R1-distill-14B's rate (0.44), a different model.
   **Why it matters** — This paragraph is used to justify the paper's most policy-relevant claim ("this holds more, not less, for stronger models"). If the 0.78 figure is a mislabeled copy of Sonnet's reversion rate rather than an independently measured V3.2 edit-following rate, the "monotonic increase with strength" claim loses one of its three anchor points and the strength-scaling story is unsupported as stated. At minimum, the reader cannot tell what condition, task, or metric ("edit-following" vs. "reversion" are different quantities throughout the paper) these three numbers were computed under, since no table, figure, or appendix reference is given for this specific triple.
   **What would address it** — Trace and correct (or explain) each of the three numbers, state the exact experimental condition and n for each, and add them to a table with confidence intervals so the claim is independently checkable.

2. **Issue** — No statement anywhere in the paper that code, data, prompts, model activations, or analysis scripts will be (or have been) released.
   **Where** — Absent throughout; the closest reference is "Model revisions, prompts, and configs are in the appendices" (Section 2), which does not commit to public release and is not verifiable from the text provided.
   **Why it matters** — This is a causal-intervention mechanistic-interpretability paper whose claims hinge on exact implementation choices (which layers form the "middle band," how attention masks are constructed, how the random control vector is drawn, how answers are parsed). Without code and data, none of the six models' results, the two-slot composition result, or the frontier API-prefill protocol can be independently reproduced or audited — a serious problem given finding #1 above suggests at least one figure may already contain an error.
   **What would address it** — A public repository with generation scripts, prompts/templates for both task families, patching/masking code, the answer parser, and raw per-item logs (not just aggregated rates) for every cell in every table.

3. **Issue** — The state-patch, restore, and planting rates in Table 1 (and elsewhere) are explicitly conditioned on "items where the text edit was followed," a rate that itself ranges from 0.44 to 0.98 across models — yet the paper treats the conditioned rates as directly comparable across models.
   **Where** — Table 1 footnote ("rates conditioned on items where the text edit was followed") and Section 3 discussion of cross-model comparisons.
   **Why it matters** — Conditioning on a post-treatment, model-specific selection variable (whether the text edit "took") can change the composition of the analyzed subsample in ways that differ by model. For R1-distill-14B, only 44% of items pass this filter before any further conditioning is applied for the planted-value or restore-state columns; for Qwen3-4B, 98% pass. If the subset of items where a text edit "worked" is systematically easier, more legible, or otherwise different from the excluded 56% (for R1-distill-14B) or 2% (for Qwen3-4B), then the planted-value rates being compared side-by-side in the same table are not estimating the same underlying population effect. The paper never reports unconditional rates or checks whether conditioning changes the conclusion.
   **What would address it** — Report both conditional and unconditional planting/restore rates, or explicitly model the selection process (e.g., inverse-probability weighting) and show the qualitative conclusions are robust to it.

4. **Issue** — The central compositionality claim — that two planted values sum "at or above the product of the single-position rates" — cannot be verified from the numbers actually given.
   **Where** — Section 3, "Two slots compose": composed rates of 0.90 (Qwen2.5-7B) and 0.88 (Qwen3-4B) are compared to "the product of the single-slot rates," but the single-slot planting rate for this specific two-counter summation task is never reported — only the composed (two-slot) rate and the general ten-step chain single-slot rates from Table 1 (a different task) are given.
   **Why it matters** — This is presented as a striking, mechanistically important finding ("a number that exists nowhere in its input, assembled from two independently planted memories"), but as written it is an assertion, not a demonstrated calculation. Using Table 1's single-slot rate (0.74 for Qwen2.5-7B) as a stand-in would predict a product of ~0.55, well below the observed 0.90 — the opposite of "at or above" — which only makes sense if the single-slot rate on the two-counter task itself is much higher than on the ten-step chain task, a number the paper never gives.
   **What would address it** — Report the single-slot planting rate measured on the exact two-counter task, alongside the composed rate, so the reader can compute and check the product claim directly.

5. **Issue** — The Section 6 "Jacobian lens" ablation, used to bound the scope of the claimed mechanism, is neither defined nor cited, and its key parameter ("the strongest dose that leaves plain text generation unchanged") is not specified.
   **Where** — Section 6: "Ablating the top concepts a Jacobian lens reads from the activations, at the strongest dose that leaves plain text generation unchanged, has no effect on any of this."
   **Why it matters** — This sentence supports a specific negative/boundary claim (the memory is "not carried by the lens-readable subspace at that dose") that a reader has no way to evaluate: what is a "Jacobian lens" technically, is it a novel method or from prior work (no citation is given, unlike every other technique in the paper), what "dose" units are used, how was the "strongest ... unchanged" threshold determined, and on how many items/models was this tested? Section 6 overall has no sample sizes, no confidence intervals, and no quantitative table, in contrast to the careful bracketed reporting in Sections 3–5.
   **What would address it** — Define or cite the Jacobian-lens method, report the ablation-strength search procedure and its stopping criterion quantitatively, and give sample sizes/CIs for all three "scope" results (capacity limit, box-tracking, lens ablation).

6. **Issue** — The claim that the causal read-through-attention "memory" mechanism itself scales with model strength is extrapolated from purely behavioral evidence on frontier models, since the mechanistic interventions (state patching, attention knockout) that establish the mechanism were never run on the frontier models.
   **Where** — Abstract ("this holds more, not less, for stronger models"); Section 1 oversight paragraph; Section 2 ("Frontier models ... are tested behaviorally through API prefill").
   **Why it matters** — API prefill on closed models permits text edits and observing continuations, but not residual-stream patching or attention masking. So for DeepSeek V3.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Llama 3.3 70B, the paper has only shown that edited text is often followed (a correlational/behavioral finding consistent with many possible mechanisms, including the very "commentary vs. computation" ambiguity the paper set out to resolve) — not that the specific attention-read-from-token-position mechanism demonstrated on 4B–14B models is what implements this behavior in frontier models. The oversight-relevant claim ("the visible trace is causally load-bearing... for stronger models") is the paper's most consequential takeaway for AI-safety readers, yet it is the least mechanistically supported.
   **What would address it** — Either soften the frontier-model claim to "behaviorally consistent with, but not mechanistically confirmed for, the same read-back mechanism," or obtain mechanistic evidence on an open-weight model at comparable scale to at least one frontier model to bridge the gap.

7. **Issue** — Sparse and inconsistent disclosure of key hyperparameters and statistical procedures needed for replication.
   **Where** — Throughout Section 2 and the tables: "a middle band of layers" (no layer indices, no per-model specification, despite models spanning very different depths); "norm-matched random perturbation" (norm computed how — per-token, per-layer, per-position? drawn from what distribution?); "2 to 3 seeds" (which, and how combined); no correction for multiple comparisons across the many model × condition cells in Tables/Sections 3–5; several conditioned cells have small n before further subsetting (e.g., R1-distill-14B n=46, R1-distill-1.5B excluded at n=12); the "24 of 24" hand-inspected subsample used to explain away the distill models' elevated random-control reversion rate (Section 3) does not describe its sampling procedure (exhaustive vs. selected); and it is never stated how "unparseable answers... logged separately" (Section 2) are handled in the denominators of the reported rates.
   **Why it matters** — Each of these is individually minor, but collectively they mean a skeptical reader cannot rerun or check any single number in the paper, and cannot rule out that some cross-model contrasts (e.g., "Qwen models never revert, gap under 0.01") are within noise once corrected for the number of comparisons made.
   **What would address it** — A methods appendix (or released config files) specifying exact layer ranges per model, the random-control sampling distribution, per-condition n after all conditioning steps, the unparseable-answer rate and handling, and a stated multiple-comparisons policy (or an argument for why none is needed, e.g., pre-registered hypotheses).

## Minor concerns

- Both referenced figures ("[Figure: knockout bars]," "[Figure: four-condition reversion, V3.2 and Sonnet]") are not present in the reviewed text, so visual claims (error bars, per-item scatter) cannot be checked.
- Most Section 5 point estimates (0.43, 0.05, 0.78, 0.97, 0.85, 0.98, 0.82, 0.90, etc.) lack the bracketed 95% intervals used consistently in Table 1 — an inconsistency in reporting rigor across sections.
- The five/twenty/forty-step depth results for Qwen3-4B (0.984, 0.974, 0.951) are given only as bare prose numbers with no table, CIs, or n, despite "chain lengths up to forty steps" being a headline abstract claim.
- "R1-distill-1.5B is excluded (12 usable items; random control exceeds the planted-value rate)" is a post-hoc exclusion of the one result that would have contradicted the thesis; transparent, but the tiny n makes the exclusion itself statistically weak evidence that this is genuinely an outlier rather than noise.
- No funding, compute-budget, or conflict-of-interest statement.
- The claim "Capacity to rederive is necessary for checking; whether it is used is a property of the model's training" (Section 5) is a strong general conclusion about training dynamics drawn from behavioral contrasts across only 4–5 frontier/mid-size models on synthetic arithmetic/narrative tasks.
- Terms "matched trace length," "strongest dose," and "middle band of layers" recur without operational definitions anywhere in the visible text.

## Verdict

**Major revision** — the causal-intervention paradigm (planting, restoring, attention-knockout, matched controls) is well-designed and the cross-model/cross-task replication is a genuine strength, but an apparent numeric inconsistency in the paper's own headline "strength scaling" claim (#1), total absence of a code/data availability statement (#2), an unverifiable compositionality claim (#4), and an undefined boundary-condition method (#5) must be resolved before the paper's central claims can be trusted or reproduced.
