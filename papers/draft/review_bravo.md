# Peer Review — Reviewer bravo

## Summary

The paper asks whether the tokens a language model writes during chain-of-thought reasoning causally carry forward the values used in later computation, or whether they are post-hoc commentary. Using arithmetic and narrative state-tracking tasks with known ground truth, the authors run three interventions: (1) planting an unwritten value into the residual stream at a written token's position ("state patch"), which drags the final answer to the planted value in 74–100% of cases while a norm-matched random vector moves answers under 1%; (2) blocking attention from later positions to the value's token, which collapses edit-following to ~0% on four models across three families while control ablations (adjacent token, random prompt token, earlier written value) leave following at baseline; and (3) testing which prompt evidence causes a model to override an edited written value, finding model-specific "checking" policies (DeepSeek V3.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Llama 3.3 70B, and small Qwen models differ sharply). The headline claim is that the written token's position functions as addressable memory read via attention, and that reliance on this written trace — rather than re-derivation — increases with model strength, which the authors argue has direct implications for chain-of-thought monitoring/oversight.

## Major concerns

1. **Issue** — Section 6 ("Scope"), which is used to bound where the entire mechanism applies, reports zero quantitative evidence — no rates, sample sizes, or confidence intervals — for claims that are otherwise central to the paper's interpretive reach.
   **Where** — Section 6, all three sentences: "models fail at one to two dependent steps (five for Llama 70B)"; "a box-tracking task with equal state size is tracked internally with a fifth of the writing, and writing does not predict its correctness"; "Ablating the top concepts a Jacobian lens reads... has no effect on any of this."
   **Why it matters** — Everywhere else in the paper (Sections 3–5), every claim is backed by a rate, an n, and a 95% bootstrap interval, and the authors are careful to distinguish tight from noisy estimates. Section 6 breaks this standard entirely at the exact point where the paper draws the boundary of its own mechanism — i.e., exactly where over- or under-claiming would matter most for a reader deciding how far to generalize the "written value is memory" result. A prose-only "has no effect on any of this" for a Jacobian-lens ablation is not falsifiable as written: no dose-response curve, no effect size, no comparison condition.
   **What would address it** — Report the same quantitative format used in Sections 3–5 for all three scope claims: item counts, rates with CIs, and the specific ablation strength/dose used for the Jacobian-lens control, ideally in a table analogous to Table 3.

2. **Issue** — No correction for multiple comparisons across the large number of point estimates reported (6 models × 4 conditions in Table 3, plus the 4-model × 4-condition grid in Section 5), combined with a post-hoc exclusion (R1-distill-1.5B) whose stated rationale mixes a legitimate reason (n=12) with an inconvenient result ("random control exceeds the planted-value rate"), and a categorical claim ("all 24 of 24") drawn from an unreported, apparently small convenience sample with no interval at all.
   **Where** — Section 3, table and surrounding text; Section 3 paragraph beginning "The distill models re-solve..."; Section 5.
   **Why it matters** — Individually, the largest effects (planted value vs. random control) are so large that multiplicity is unlikely to matter. But the paper also leans on many finer-grained, smaller-magnitude contrasts (e.g., "gap under 0.01 between conditions" for Qwen never reverting; the composition experiment "matching the product of single-slot rates") to support secondary claims about model-specific checking policies — exactly the regime where uncorrected multiple comparisons and selective exclusion are most likely to mislead. Excluding R1-distill-1.5B partly because it contradicts the pattern (rather than purely for n=12) is a red flag for selective reporting, and readers cannot tell whether other borderline cases were also tried and dropped.
   **What would address it** — State a pre-registered exclusion criterion (e.g., n < threshold, decided before seeing outcomes) and report excluded conditions with their raw numbers rather than omitting them; apply a multiple-comparison correction (or a hierarchical model) across the full grid of contrasts; give a CI (or explicitly label as illustrative/anecdotal) for the "24 of 24" claim.

3. **Issue** — Only 2–3 random seeds underlie most headline numbers, and it is not specified whether the reported 95% bootstrap intervals resample at the item level, the seed level, or both, despite items within a seed plausibly sharing correlated noise (same checkpoint, same sampling stream).
   **Where** — Section 2 ("Sample sizes are 100 to 600 per condition with 2 to 3 seeds; error bars are 95 percent bootstrap intervals"); Table 3 (e.g., Qwen3-4B planted-value CI of [0.99, 1.00] on apparently ~100 conditioned items).
   **Why it matters** — With only 2–3 seeds, between-seed variance (a component distinct from item-sampling variance) is essentially unestimable, and an item-level-only bootstrap would understate true uncertainty, producing CIs that look more precise than the underlying design supports. This matters specifically for the paper's finer claims (e.g., differentiating 0.95 from 0.98 across chain depths, or treating a 0.01 "mixed sums" rate as evidence of independence rather than noise).
   **What would address it** — Report the resampling unit explicitly; if bootstrapping over items only, add a seed-level sensitivity analysis or a mixed-effects model with seed as a random effect; increase seed count for the headline comparisons that drive the paper's strongest claims.

4. **Issue** — The oversight-relevant claim that edit-following "increases with model strength... this holds more, not less, for stronger models" is supported only by purely behavioral API-prefill edit-following rates on closed frontier models (DeepSeek V3.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Llama 3.3 70B), while the actual mechanistic evidence (state patching, attention knockout establishing attention-mediated read-back) is confined to open 4B–14B models. These are two non-overlapping populations, and the paper's central mechanistic claim is silently transferred from one to the other.
   **Where** — Abstract ("this holds more, not less, for stronger models"); Section 1, closing paragraph ("edit-following on such values increases with model strength (R1-distill-7B 0.42, DeepSeek V3.2 0.78, Claude Sonnet 4.5 0.97...)"); Discussion.
   **Why it matters** — A monotone trend in one behavioral metric (edit-following) across three points does not establish that the same causal pathway (attention reading a residual-stream slot at the written token) is what produces that behavior in closed models whose internals were never probed. The paper's own framing in Sections 3–4 is explicitly mechanistic ("the written token's position... behaves as memory that later computation reads through attention"); extending this specific mechanistic language to frontier models based on behavior alone is an evidentiary overreach. Methodological guidance on activation patching (e.g., Heimersheim & Nanda [2404.15255]) stresses that patching results license claims about the specific circuit intervened on, not claims generalized to systems where no intervention was performed — the same caution should apply here to the frontier-model extrapolation.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly separate "mechanistic" claims (backed by white-box intervention, 4B–14B only) from "behavioral/correlational" claims (frontier models, edit-following rate only) in the abstract and discussion; soften the abstract's "this holds more, not less, for stronger models" to make clear it is a behavioral trend, not a demonstrated mechanism, in the frontier tier.

5. **Issue** — The only control offered for the state-patch manipulation is a "norm-matched random vector"; there is no control using a structured-but-semantically-wrong vector (e.g., a valid activation pattern for a different, incorrect arithmetic operation, or a shuffled/permuted real written-value vector) to rule out that the effect is driven by "any plausible arithmetic-value-shaped activation" rather than specifically the semantic content the authors attribute to it.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("state patch... overwrites the residual stream... with activations captured from a run in which a different value was written there"); Section 3 (random control described only as norm-matched random).
   **Why it matters** — A random vector of matched norm is a weak alternative hypothesis (pure noise); if it fails to move the answer, that only shows the effect isn't explained by magnitude/norm alone, not that the effect is specifically the *correct semantic value* rather than any in-distribution, structurally valid activation at that position. This matters because the paper's strongest claims (compositionality of two independently planted values summing correctly) depend on the planted state carrying precise numeric content, not merely "looking like a real written-value activation."
   **What would address it** — Add a control that plants a real, in-distribution written-value activation for a *different* variable/step (already partially present in the "earlier written value" attention-knockout control in Section 4, but not as a state-patch control in Section 3) to isolate content-specificity from mere distributional plausibility.

6. **Issue** — Key hyperparameters of the causal interventions — how many layers form the "middle band" patched in the state-patch experiment, how that band was chosen, and whether it was tuned to maximize effect size — are not reported in the text and are deferred to unincluded appendices, along with code/data availability.
   **Where** — Section 2 ("overwrites the residual stream at the value's token position across a middle band of layers"); Section 2 ("Model revisions, prompts, and configs are in the appendices").
   **Why it matters** — In an intervention-heavy causal study, the choice of which layers to patch is itself a researcher degree of freedom; if the band was selected by observing which layers produce the largest effect, the headline numbers (74–100%) could be inflated relative to a pre-specified band. No statement of code or data release is given anywhere in the reviewed text, which is a significant reproducibility gap for a paper whose evidentiary core is a specific intervention protocol.
   **What would address it** — Report the exact layer range per model, the procedure used to select it (grid search on a held-out set vs. fixed a priori choice), and a link to code/data, or an explicit statement that release is pending.

7. **Issue** — R1-distill-7B's edit-following rate is reported as 0.60 in Table 3, but as 0.42 in the Section 1 oversight paragraph under a different, unreconciled protocol label ("on the same protocol through API prefill").
   **Where** — Section 3, Table 3 (R1-distill-7B, "text edit followed" = 0.60 [0.53, 0.66]); Section 1, closing paragraph ("R1-distill-7B 0.42, DeepSeek V3.2 0.78, Claude Sonnet 4.5 0.97 on the same protocol through API prefill").
   **Why it matters** — These appear to be two different measurements (white-box run vs. API-prefill behavioral run) of a related but not identical quantity for the same model, presented back-to-back in the paper's framing of the model-strength trend, without ever being distinguished or reconciled for the reader. As currently written, a careful reader cannot tell if this is an inconsistency, a different metric, or a typo, which undermines confidence in the one data point that bridges the white-box and frontier-behavioral halves of the argument.
   **What would address it** — Explicitly label the two numbers as distinct metrics/protocols (e.g., "text-edit-followed" vs. "edit-following on cheaply-nonregenerable values via API prefill") the first time each appears, and clarify why R1-distill-7B is included in a comparison series alongside proprietary frontier models given it is also the model most affected by the confounding "re-solve" behavior documented earlier in Section 3.

## Minor concerns

- Notation is inconsistent between percent ("74 to 100 percent") in the abstract and raw decimals ("0.643 to 0.000") in the body; pick one convention.
- The table caption clarifies that most columns are "conditioned on items where the text edit was followed," but this is easy to miss and effective per-cell denominators (after conditioning) are never given alongside the reported n.
- "Checking requires capacity, and capacity does not imply checking... whether it is used is a property of the model's training" (Section 5) is a causal claim about training that the paper has no direct evidence for (no training-data or training-procedure analysis was performed); should be softened to an observed association.
- The related-work section (Section 7) cites a dense run of arXiv IDs (e.g., 2606.10646, 2604.23351, 2603.01437, 2602.15868) without author names, venues, or enough description to let a reader independently judge priority claims; a recent related causal study on scratchpad-vs-weight reliance under RL training (Kshirsagar, "The Weight of Silence" [2607.20952]) suggests reliance on written traces vs. internal weights can shift with training regime — relevant context for the paper's claim that trace-reliance is fixed "by training" but not discussed.
- "d signed two-digit additions and subtractions" (Section 2) introduces the variable d without stating its experimental range inline (chain lengths appear only later, scattered across sections).
- Figure references ("[Figure: knockout bars]", "[Figure: four-condition reversion, V3.2 and Sonnet]") are placeholders in the provided text; actual figures were not available for review.

## Verdict

**Major revision** — the core causal-intervention results (Sections 3–4) are well-powered and convincingly controlled, but the paper's scope-bounding evidence (Section 6) is entirely unquantified, several headline claims lack multiple-comparison correction or a principled treatment of small/excluded samples, and the extrapolation from white-box mechanistic results (4B–14B) to a mechanistic-sounding claim about frontier proprietary models rests on behavioral correlation alone.
