### Summary

The paper makes three linked claims: (1) a value planted into the residual stream at a written CoT token — never appearing in the visible text — propagates into the final answer at high rates (0.74–1.00) across six open models and two task formats, while norm-matched random noise does not (≤0.03); (2) this propagation runs specifically through attention from later positions back to that token, since blocking that attention edge (but not neighboring/random/earlier-value edges) collapses following to ~0; (3) whether a model checks the written value against re-derivable prompt evidence is model- and evidence-type-specific, with DeepSeek V3.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Llama 3.3 70B showing three different policies. The paper is unusually well-quantified by AF/interp standards — CIs everywhere, matched controls, a real dissociation (illegible-token vs. attention-block) — and I think the core "planted value propagates, random noise doesn't" result is probably real. But the mechanistic upgrade from that result to "addressable memory read through attention" rests on interventions (a "middle band of layers," attention blocked "at all layers") that are never localized or swept, so the paper cannot currently distinguish a genuine retrieval circuit from wholesale denial of a token's contribution to the forward pass. Combine that with a headline "holds more, not less, for stronger models" claim built on three cherry-picked, protocol-mismatched points that a fourth already-collected data point (Llama) contradicts, a completely undefined method ("Jacobian lens") used to bound the paper's scope, a suspiciously clean 24/24 post-hoc explanation for an inconvenient control result, and zero code/seed/decoding-parameter disclosure, and I don't think the reader should treat the mechanistic story as established yet — only the coarser correlational one.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — the abstract's claim that trace-dependence "holds more, not less, for stronger models" is built from a 3-point, protocol-confounded comparison that omits a 4th already-reported data point (Llama 3.3 70B) that contradicts it. `[Narrative — overclaiming]`
   **Where** — Abstract, final sentence; Section 1, last paragraph ("R1-distill-7B 0.42, DeepSeek V3.2 0.78, Claude Sonnet 4.5 0.97"); contrast with Section 5 ("Llama 3.3 70B follows edits at 0.97 in both the deep and the defined conditions").
   **Why it matters** — Llama 3.3 70B is reported elsewhere in the same paper at 0.97 edit-following in the deep (non-regenerable) condition — tied with Sonnet at the ceiling, despite Llama generally ranking below both Sonnet 4.5 and V3.2 on public reasoning benchmarks. Slotted into the "strength" list it breaks monotonicity; left out, the three remaining points look like a clean trend. A reader who only reads the abstract walks away believing a general law about model strength and oversight risk that the paper's own Section 5 numbers do not support once all four frontier/near-frontier data points it collected are used.
   **What would address it** — Either put all four points (R1-distill-7B, V3.2, Llama, Sonnet) on the same axis and report the correlation honestly (probably weak/non-monotonic), or drop the "more, not less" claim to something scoped and hedged: "policy differs by model; we did not find it monotonic in general capability."

2. **Issue** — the framing "we answer this causally" undersells that two cited papers already ran causal interventions on written CoT tokens with comparable following rates. `[Novelty]`
   **Where** — Abstract/Intro ("We answer this causally…"); Section 7, "Closest to this work" paragraph.
   **Why it matters** — I checked both: `[2606.29522]` ("Do Models Read What They Write? Causal Registers in Scratchpad Reasoning," Shih/Winnicki/Darve) already patches internal scratchpad representations and reports downstream following at 0.80–0.91; `[2505.04955]` ("Chain-of-Thought Tokens are Computer Program Variables," Zhu/Wang/Sui) already intervenes on value-storing CoT tokens in multiplication/DP tasks. Both are accurately characterized in Section 7, so this is not a missing-citation problem — but the framing in the abstract and intro reads as if the causal question were open, when it was already answered at similar effect sizes on a narrower model set. The genuinely new material here (cross-family generalization on *unmodified* pretrained models, attention-edge localization, slot composition, the checking-policy taxonomy) is real, but it's an extension of an established causal result, not the first causal test of the write→read link. The informed-reader update should be scoped accordingly: raise P(the causal-register finding generalizes across unmodified model families) — not P(we now know written CoT tokens are causally load-bearing), which a naive abstract-only reader would take away.
   **What would address it** — One sentence in the intro conceding the causal question was already answered at smaller scale, with the contribution reframed explicitly as "generalizes X, localizes Y, adds Z" (which is what Section 7 already argues, just too late).

3. **Issue** — the explanation for distill models' anomalous random-control reversion rate (0.21–0.30, vs. ≤0.01 for instruct models) rests on a manually-coded "24 of 24" sample with no stated denominator. `[Evidence — cherry-picking / post-hoc]`
   **Where** — Section 3, table note ("in a text-logged sample, all 24 of 24 reverting random-control continuations re-derive from the prompt's starting value…").
   **Why it matters** — This number is doing real work: without it, R1-distill's random-control baseline (0.21–0.30) looks uncomfortably close to its planted-value rate (0.70–0.76), which would undercut the "random noise doesn't move the answer, planted values do" story that anchors the whole paper. Given rates and n (R1-distill-7B n=65 @0.30, R1-distill-14B n=46 @0.21), the total pool of reverting random-control cases across both models is roughly 30, not 24 — so "24 of 24" is a subsample, and the paper doesn't say how it was selected, whether it's exhaustive of what was hand-checked, or whether any borderline cases were dropped from the 24. A perfect 100% on a manually-judged categorical call that exactly resolves an inconvenient number is exactly the shape a post-hoc-shaped explanation takes.
   **Why it matters (2)** — this is also the exact hole a maximally adversarial reading should press: "re-solving, not in-place correction" is the interpretation that saves the thesis, and it's checked by hand on a convenience sample, not automatically on the full reverting population.
   **What would address it** — Report the full denominator (all reverting random-control cases, not a sub-sample), and ideally an automated classifier (e.g., regex/parse for "let me restart" vs. edited-value continuation) applied to 100% of cases, with inter-rater or classifier-vs-human agreement reported.

4. **Issue** — the attention-knockout result (following → 0.000) is not checked against the alternative that blocking all attention to a token, at all layers, simply derails generation generally rather than specifically blocking value retrieval. `[Evidence — alternative explanation]`
   **Where** — Section 4 (attention block description; "collapses following on every model tested… to 0.000").
   **Why it matters** — The paper logs unparseable answers "separately" for the base protocol (Section 2) but never reports the unparseable/incoherent-output rate under the knockout condition specifically. If blocking attention to a single token at every layer of the network tends to produce garbled or off-distribution continuations, then "following drops to 0" is consistent with "the model can no longer speak coherently near that position" as much as with "the model can no longer retrieve the specific value" — these are different mechanistic claims with different oversight implications (the latter is a precise "delete this memory slot" story, the former is "we broke the model"). The illegible-token dissociation (Section 4) partially addresses this for a different manipulation, but not for the attention block itself.
   **What would address it** — Report output fluency/perplexity or unparseable-rate under the knockout condition, and ideally show the model still solves an *unrelated* downstream sub-task correctly in the same continuation, to demonstrate the damage is localized to the blocked value rather than global.

5. **Issue** — the Section 6 scope claim ("the memory studied here is not carried by the lens-readable subspace") rests on an "ablating the top concepts a Jacobian lens reads" method that is never defined or cited anywhere in the paper. `[Reproducibility / Presentation]`
   **Where** — Section 6, final sentence.
   **Why it matters** — This is not a minor terminology gap: it's the paper's only stated boundary result distinguishing its "addressable memory" claim from lens-based interpretability findings, and as written it is unfalsifiable — no reader can tell what a "Jacobian lens" is, what "top concepts" or "strongest dose that leaves plain text generation unchanged" mean operationally, or whether this is a known technique from a cited method or something invented for this paper. A claim this load-bearing for scoping the contribution needs at minimum a citation or a one-sentence definition.
   **What would address it** — Define the method or cite its source; report the dose-response curve it implies ("strongest dose that leaves generation unchanged") rather than asserting the endpoint.

6. **Issue** — no code, model weights/prompts beyond appendices, seeds, or decoding parameters (temperature/sampling vs. greedy) are given anywhere in the main text. `[Reproducibility]`
   **Where** — Section 2 (protocol description) and throughout; "Model revisions, prompts, and configs are in the appendices" is the only pointer, and appendices are not part of the reviewed text.
   **Why it matters** — The paper reports "sampling noise floor… is 0.00 throughout" for no-edit truncations, which is the kind of number that depends entirely on decoding settings (near-deterministic under greedy decoding, noisier under temperature sampling) and is never stated. Without decoding parameters, a competent grad student cannot replicate even the baseline noise floor, let alone the causal patch results. There's also no mention of code or artifact release at all — not even a "code will be released" placeholder — which is itself informative given how central the exact patch band and mask implementation are to what's being claimed.
   **What would address it** — State decoding parameters in the main text (not just appendix), and commit to (or provide) a code/patch-script release, since the state-patch and attention-mask implementations are themselves the paper's core technical contribution.

7. **Issue** — neither the state-patch's "middle band of layers" nor the attention-block's "at all layers" scope is ever swept or localized; the paper has no layer-resolved ablation for either intervention, despite the entire mechanistic claim ("the state at a written token is addressable memory… read through attention") being a claim about *where in the network* this happens. `[Evidence — missing ablations]`
   **Where** — Section 2 (intervention definitions: "a middle band of layers," "at all layers"); Sections 3–4 (results); Section 8 admits the adjacent issue ("the patch overwrites the full residual band rather than an isolated value subspace") without acting on it.
   **Why it matters** — This is the single ablation that would let the paper's central metaphor ("addressable memory," "read through attention") actually mean something mechanistically specific. Right now: (a) the state patch overwrites an unspecified multi-layer band, so we don't know if the "memory" lives in a few late layers (consistent with a genuine retrieval/write circuit) or is smeared everywhere (consistent with something closer to just re-running the model on different input, which is a much less interesting claim); (b) the attention block masks *every layer*, so a positive result ("following → 0") cannot distinguish "there's a specific attention-mediated read edge" from "deleting a token's entire causal contribution at every layer breaks anything that depended on it, trivially, regardless of mechanism." The paper does run a robustness sweep along a different axis (chain length: 5/20/40 steps for the state patch), which shows they know how to do sensitivity analysis — they just didn't apply it to the one axis (layer depth) that the mechanistic claim actually turns on. `[2404.15255]` (Heimersheim & Nanda, "How to use and interpret activation patching") is directly on point here: their central methodological advice is to prefer minimal, localized patches and to check results survive alternative patch scopes, precisely because broad patches conflate "this component matters" with "I deleted a lot of the network's information at that position."
   **What would address it** — A layer sweep for both interventions (single-layer patches/masks, patches restricted to early/mid/late thirds, and a minimal-sufficient-band search), reporting whether following-rate is concentrated in a small layer range or requires the full band/full depth to reproduce. This is the ablation the paper's title claim needs and currently lacks.

### Minor concerns

- Section 3 table: unclear whether "n" is the same sample across the "text edit followed," "restore correct state," and "plant never-written value" columns, or whether each column draws its own conditioned sub-sample — the text says rates are "conditioned on items where the text edit was followed" but doesn't clarify if restore/plant experiments reuse those exact items or a fresh draw.
- Section 4: OLMo-2-7B's unusually low baseline ("whose baseline is low in this prompt format but whose contrast is intact") is asserted rather than explained — why does this prompt format specifically depress OLMo's baseline, and does that call the cross-model comparability of Section 4's numbers into question?
- Section 6: "models fail at one to two dependent steps (five for Llama 70B)" without writing is stated with no accompanying table/n, unlike every other numeric claim in the paper — inconsistent level of evidentiary support within the same section.
- The abstract number "R1-distill-7B 0.42" for "state a model cannot cheaply regenerate" and Table 3's "R1-distill-7B" row (0.60 text-edit-followed) are different quantities from different experiments but share a bare model name with no in-text disambiguation — easy to conflate on a skim.
- No units/decoding info for "2 to 3 seeds" — which specific seeds, and is the variance across those 2–3 seeds itself reported anywhere, or only the bootstrap CI within a single run?
- Checked and found no issue: the random-norm vs. planted-value control design (Section 3) and the matched-token-type controls for the attention block (adjacent/random-operand/earlier-value, Section 4) are reasonably matched, non-strawman controls — this is a case where "baselines" produced no independent concern beyond what's captured in concerns 4 and 7 above.
- Checked and found no issue: no p-value-tier problems — the paper reports 95% bootstrap CIs throughout rather than leaning on point-estimate p-values, which is good practice and largely inoculates it against the classic .01<p<.05 concern.

### Belief update

Modest, upward, and narrower than the abstract wants. Before this paper my estimate of P(planted/edited CoT state causally overrides both the visible text and the "true" derivable value, across unmodified pretrained models, not just fine-tuned ones) was already elevated by `[2606.29522]` and `[2505.04955]`; this paper pushes it up further and extends it to cross-family generalization, which is a real and useful data point. What it does *not* move for me yet is the more specific mechanistic claim in the title — "addressable memory... read through attention" — because the interventions supporting that specific framing (broad-band residual patch, full-depth attention mask) aren't localized enough to rule out cruder explanations (concern 7), and the paper's own most quotable claim ("holds more, not less, for stronger models") doesn't survive including a data point the authors already collected (concern 1). Net: my confidence that "written CoT values are sometimes causally load-bearing memory, not mere commentary, especially when not cheaply re-derivable" went up meaningfully; my confidence in the specific attention-read-back circuit story and in a strength-monotonic oversight risk went up only slightly, pending the missing layer ablation.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the core "planted value ≠ random noise" result is credible and multi-pronged, but the paper's title-level mechanistic claim needs the layer-localized ablation (concern 7) before "addressable memory read through attention" is distinguishable from "we deleted a token's entire contribution to the forward pass and, unsurprisingly, that mattered."
